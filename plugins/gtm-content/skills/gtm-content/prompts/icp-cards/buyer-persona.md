# Buyer role profile

A profile of one role in the buying group: what their job is really judged on, what wears them down, how they buy, and what makes them walk away. Reps use it to prepare calls and rewrite outreach; marketers use it to pick language. Under 500 words.

## Prompt

```
Brief: write a profile of one buyer role. Draw only on the material below. Missing pieces become [ASK: what is missing].

- Role label: [a description of the role, never an invented first name, e.g. "first RevOps hire at a scaling software company"]
- Titles this person actually holds: [the exact job titles]
- Where they work: [industry, size, stage]
- What they own: [their area of responsibility]
- How their boss judges them: [the numbers or outcomes in their review]
- The grind: [the recurring, weekly frustration]
- The worry: [the bigger problem that keeps coming back in planning]
- Today's workaround: [the tool, spreadsheet, agency or habit they use now]
- Where the workaround breaks: [the gap that opens a door for us]
- Where they learn: [newsletters, communities, podcasts, people they follow]
- How they buy: [self-serve trial / demo first / committee / internal champion]
- What makes them distrust a vendor: [behaviours or claims that end the conversation]
- What they push back with: [three to five objections]
- Their own words: [verbatim phrases from calls, reviews, surveys or tickets, each with its source]

Organise the profile under these headings:
- Who this is: label, titles, company context.
- What the job is really about: ownership, how they are measured, what a normal week looks like.
- What hurts: the weekly grind and the strategic worry, using their own words where supplied.
- How they get by now: the workaround and what it costs.
- What good looks like to them: the outcome if the problem went away.
- How a deal happens: steps, usual length, who else gets involved.
- What earns trust: the message and the kind of proof that moves this person.
- What ends it: objections as they would phrase them, red flags, deal breakers.
- Where to find them: channels, formats, tone.

Concrete and short. A rep reading it before a call should change at least one question they plan to ask.
```

## Rules for this draft

- Quotation marks are only for real phrases from the material. Paraphrase without them.
- Built without real buyer input (calls, interviews, reviews)? Then it is a hypothesis: say so in the first line, and list the three or four questions that would confirm or kill it.
- Never invent a demographic detail (age, background, personal life). Stick to the job.

## Check before handing back

- Would someone in this role recognise themselves, or does it read like a horoscope?
- Are the objections written in the buyer's voice rather than ours?
- Does the trust section name specific kinds of proof, for example "a reference at a company of the same stage" rather than "social proof"?

## Hand back

The file path, the role label, and any gaps. Suggest the user checks the profile against their next few discovery calls and updates it with what buyers actually say.
