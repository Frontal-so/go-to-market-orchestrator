# Team win recap

An internal note about something that went right: a deal closed, a churn risk saved, a launch shipped, a process fixed. The point is not applause. It is a record precise enough that a colleague could repeat the win next quarter. Under 500 words.

## Prompt

```
Brief: write an internal recap of a team win for colleagues who were not there. Use only what is below. Where a detail is missing, write [ASK: the missing detail] and move on.

- Team: [team or company name]
- Kind of win: [deal / save / launch / process change / partnership / other]
- In one line, what happened: [summary]
- When: [date or period]
- Who took part: [each person and what their role was]
- How it unfolded: [the key steps and decisions, in order]
- The turning point: [the one or two moves that decided it]
- The measurable outcome: [figures with their period: revenue, retention, hours saved, cycle time]
- What we would keep doing: [habits, plays, assets that worked]
- What we would change: [one honest reflection, even if small]
- Where this will be shared: [channel, meeting or wiki page]

Shape of the recap:
- A title that says what was won, specifically. Example shape: "Renewal saved at a 40-seat logistics account after a two-week rescue plan".
- Three bullets for readers with thirty seconds.
- Background: why this was hard or why it mattered, in a short paragraph.
- The play, step by step: a short narrative that names who did what and when.
- Outcome: the supplied figures, as a short list.
- Lessons: three, each with a few words of label and two or three sentences of explanation.
- Make it standard: the specific actions worth turning into the default.
- Thanks: one sentence per person naming the exact thing they did.

Plain words, no hype. Written so a new hire could follow the same steps.
```

## Rules for this draft

- Name only the people the user named, and credit only the contributions they described. Never guess who did what.
- Commercial terms (price, discount, contract length) appear only if the user included them.
- A customer is referred to the way the team already does internally.

## Check before handing back

- Is each lesson something a person could act on, rather than a slogan?
- Does the outcome section contain only supplied numbers?
- Would every person thanked recognise their contribution as described?

## Hand back

The file path, the three summary bullets, and any `[ASK: ...]` gaps. The user shares it with the team after the people named have read it.
