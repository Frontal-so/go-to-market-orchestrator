# Long-form customer story

For the buyer who is close to a decision and wants to see, in detail, that a company like theirs got a result like the one they need. It lives on a website, inside a proposal, or in a follow-up email after a late-stage call. Length: roughly 600 to 900 words.

## Prompt

```
Brief: draft a long-form customer story. Treat the material below as the only source of truth. If a field is blank or vague, put [ASK: the missing fact] at that spot in the draft and keep going.

THE CUSTOMER
- How to refer to them: [real name only if they agreed in writing to be named; otherwise a description, e.g. "a 300-person freight broker in the Midwest"]
- What they do, how big they are, where: [details]
- The people involved on their side: [roles, and names only with permission]

THE STORY
- Life before: [what was not working, how often it hurt, what it cost them in time, money, risk or customers]
- The moment they decided to act: [the event or deadline that made it a priority]
- How they chose: [what else they looked at, and the reason they picked us]
- What we did together: [the work, in order, with dates or weeks]
- First sign it was working: [the earliest win and when it happened]

THE PROOF
- Numbers: [each metric as before, after, period, and how it was measured]
- Things that changed but are hard to count: [what is easier or newly possible]
- Quotes: [exact words, speaker role, and confirmation the customer approved them]
- What happens next for them: [renewal, rollout to more teams, a new use case]

ABOUT US
- Our company and what we sold or delivered: [name, product or service]

Write it as a story a skeptical reader can check:
- Title that carries the strongest supplied result and its timeframe. Example shape: "How a regional freight broker halved quote turnaround in one quarter".
- A short line under the title saying who the customer is and what changed.
- A fact box: industry, size, region, what they used us for.
- Before: the problem told through a concrete day or moment, then what it cost.
- The decision: why they acted then and why they chose us, in their terms.
- The work: what was done, in sequence, with the dates given.
- After: lead with a block of the supplied numbers (three to five, never padded), then explain what they mean.
- Their voice: the approved quote, set apart from the text.
- Next chapter: one paragraph.
- Two sentences about us.

Every number keeps its period and its comparison. Never combine or round figures into a bigger claim. No adjective that a reader could not verify.
```

## Rules for this draft

- No approved quote supplied: the quote block reads `[ASK: approved quote from the customer's main contact]`. Never write a sample or "typical" quote.
- No written permission to name the customer: use the description, and keep the real name out of the file entirely.
- Fewer than three numbers is fine. Show what exists; do not invent a third.
- Close the file with a checklist of every open `[ASK: ...]`.

## Check before handing back

- Could each sentence be traced to a line in the brief? Delete any that could not.
- Does the title number match the number in the results block exactly?
- Is the before state specific enough that a reader in the same job would recognise it?

## Hand back

The file path, the headline, and the list of gaps. Remind the user that the customer signs off every number, quote and the use of their name before this is published.
