# Proof sheet (short case study)

A single page that proves one result fast. It gets dropped into a sales call, attached to a proposal, or linked from a website. A buyer should get the point in under a minute. Under 300 words.

## Prompt

```
Brief: write a one-page proof sheet about one customer result. Use only the material below; a gap becomes [ASK: what is missing].

- The customer: [real name only with their written OK, otherwise a description like "a mid-size cold-chain distributor"]
- Their profile: [industry, size, and region if it matters]
- What we provided: [product or service]
- The problem they had: [specific and concrete]
- Why us: [one or two reasons, as they put it]
- What we did: [a short summary]
- How long it took: [weeks or months]
- Results: [each with before, after and the period]
- Quote: [exact words, speaker role, and confirmation the customer approved it]
- Where they are now: [one sentence on how they use it today]

Page layout:
- Top line: the customer and the result. Example shape: "A cold-chain distributor cut missed deliveries by a third in eight weeks".
- Problem: two or three sentences.
- What we did: two or three sentences.
- Numbers: up to three supplied results in a stat strip, each labelled with its period.
- Quote: attributed, or [ASK: approved quote].
- Today: one short paragraph.
- About us: two sentences.

Lead with the outcome. Each line should give a buyer in a similar company a reason to believe it could work for them, without going past what the material supports.
```

## Rules for this draft

- The result in the top line is one of the supplied numbers, unchanged.
- No permission to name the customer: keep the name out of the file, not just off the page.
- No approved quote: leave the placeholder; never draft a quote for the customer.

## Check before handing back

- Under 300 words?
- Is each stat labelled with what it measures and over what period?
- Would the page still make sense if someone read only the top line and the stat strip?

## Hand back

The file path and the open gaps. The customer approves the numbers, the quote and the use of their name before the page leaves the company.
