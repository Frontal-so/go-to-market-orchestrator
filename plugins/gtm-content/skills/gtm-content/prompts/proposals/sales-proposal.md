# Post-discovery proposal

The document a seller sends after discovery calls. A good one reads like proof that the seller listened: it restates the buyer's situation in the buyer's words, ties every part of the offer to something they said, and makes the next step obvious. Under 800 words.

## Prompt

```
Brief: write a sales proposal from the discovery material below. Use only this material and any call notes or transcript the user points to. A gap becomes [ASK: what is missing].

- From: [our company and what we are proposing]
- To: [buyer company, contact name and title]
- Date: [date, or "to be filled"]
- What discovery showed: [facts, problems and goals from the calls, or the path to the notes]
- What they want to achieve: [their goals, in their words where possible]
- What we propose: [the package, product or engagement]
- Included: [deliverables, access, services]
- Not included: [explicit exclusions]
- Timeline: [start, milestones, end or renewal]
- Price and terms: [amount, payment terms, billing schedule]
- Why start now: [the reason, from what the buyer said]
- Risk reducers: [pilot, service levels, onboarding help, or "none"]
- What has to happen next: [steps to move forward]

Proposal sections:
1. Cover: to, from, date, title.
2. Where you are: three to five sentences on their situation, their goal and our proposal, in their language.
3. The problem: two or three paragraphs, including what it costs to wait if the buyer said so or the numbers to work it out were supplied.
4. What we propose: each element mapped to a need heard in discovery. Example: "You said onboarding a new rep takes six weeks; the playbook library is aimed at that."
5. Scope: included and not included, as a two-column table.
6. Timeline: phase, what happens, target date.
7. Price: the amount, the payment terms, the billing schedule.
8. Why now: the business case for starting this quarter.
9. Next steps: numbered, each with an owner and a date.

Write to the buyer as "you". Every paragraph in sections 2 to 4 points back to something from discovery.
```

## Rules for this draft

- No assumptions about the buyer's business. A cost of waiting goes in only if the buyer stated it or the user gave the numbers; show the calculation.
- Price and terms copied exactly. No discount, guarantee or deadline the user did not give.
- No vague value claims ("unlock growth", "drive efficiency").

## Check before handing back

- Would the buyer recognise their own words in section 2?
- Is every proposed element tied to a stated need?
- Is the scope table clear enough to settle a dispute later?

## Hand back

The file path and the gaps. The user reviews it and sends it from their own email or proposal tool. Updating the deal in the CRM is a separate step the user does or asks for (the gtm-guard plugin asks before any CRM write).
