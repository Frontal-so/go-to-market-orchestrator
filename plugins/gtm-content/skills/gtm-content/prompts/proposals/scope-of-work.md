# Statement of work

The document that pins down an engagement: what will be delivered, what will not, what each side has to do, how work is signed off, and how changes are handled. Its job is to prevent arguments three months in. It should be readable without a lawyer.

## Prompt

```
Brief: write a statement of work from the material below. Use only this material. A gap becomes [ASK: what is missing].

- Parties: [our company] and [client company]
- Engagement name: [title]
- Start date: [date, or "to be filled"]
- In one or two sentences: [what the engagement is]
- Goals: [two to four specific objectives]
- Deliverables: [each one with a short description]
- Out of scope: [anything that might be assumed but is not included]
- What the client provides: [access, materials, approvals, people]
- Phases: [name, what happens, duration or date], repeated per phase
- Sign-off: [how each deliverable is reviewed and accepted, and the review window]
- Changes: [how a change is requested, priced and approved]
- Rhythm: [meeting cadence, main contacts, feedback turnaround]
- Fees: [total, milestones or recurring billing, invoicing terms]
- Assumptions: [conditions this scope depends on]

Document sections:
1. Header: engagement name, parties, date, version.
2. Overview: goal and summary in two or three sentences.
3. Deliverables: numbered, each with a description.
4. Not included: a list.
5. Client commitments: what the client agrees to provide or do, and by when.
6. Schedule: a table of phase, what happens, date.
7. Sign-off: how work is reviewed and accepted. Example shape: "The client gives written feedback within five business days of each delivery." Use only the terms supplied.
8. Fees: total, schedule, invoicing.
9. Changes: the procedure for adding or swapping work.
10. Assumptions and dependencies.

Plain language and tables. No legalese.
```

## Rules for this draft

- Every deliverable must be checkable as done or not done. When the material gives a vague one ("ongoing support"), rewrite it into something measurable and flag the rewrite with `[ASK: confirm this wording]`.
- No terms the user did not give: no penalty clauses, warranties or liability language invented to fill a gap.
- This is a business document, not legal advice. The user has it reviewed by whoever reviews their contracts before signing.

## Check before handing back

- Does the out-of-scope list cover the things a client would most likely assume are included?
- Are the client's commitments as specific as ours?
- Could a third party tell from section 7 whether a deliverable was accepted?

## Hand back

The file path and the gaps. The user walks the client through it before it is attached to the contract.
