# Point-of-view carousel (LinkedIn)

A ten-slide argument: the author takes a position on something their audience gets wrong and backs it with reasons the audience can check. Plus the post text above the slides.

## Prompt

```
Brief: write a ten-slide LinkedIn carousel that argues one point of view, and its post text, from the material below.

- Author: [name and role or area of expertise]
- The position, in one sentence: [the argument]
- What most of the audience believes instead: [the view being challenged or sharpened]
- The support: [three to five reasons: data with its source, first-hand experience, a pattern the author has seen across clients]
- Who should stop on slide 1: [the target reader]
- Voice: [blunt / warm / analytical / contrarian]
- The one idea to remember: [the takeaway]

Slide plan:
1. A claim or question that makes the target reader stop. Example shape: "Your ICP is a spreadsheet, not a decision."
2. The setup: what most people believe and why it seems reasonable.
3 to 8. One reason per slide, each building on the last.
9. The whole idea in one statement, framework or simple diagram.
10. One request: a question to answer in the comments, a resource to ask for, or a reason to follow that is specific to the topic.

For each slide give: the slide number, a headline of eight words or fewer, two to five lines of body text as they would appear on the slide, and one sentence of visual direction.

Post text: three to five short lines that state the position and who it is for.
```

## Rules for this draft

- Only the support in the material. Every statistic keeps a short source line on its slide. Where an argument needs a number that was not supplied, write `[ASK: number and its source]`.
- Any term of art gets a plain-English gloss the first time it appears.
- "Follow me for more" on its own is not a request; tie it to the topic.
- Disagree with ideas, never with named people or companies.

## Check before handing back

- Does slide 1 stand alone as an image?
- Does each reason slide make exactly one point?
- Would a reader who disagrees still find the argument fair?

## Hand back

The file path, the slide 1 line, and the gaps. The user designs, exports a PDF and posts it themselves.
