# Step-by-step carousel (LinkedIn)

A carousel that teaches one process well enough that a reader could do it this week. Eight to twelve slides, one step per slide, plus the post text above them.

## Prompt

```
Brief: write a LinkedIn how-to carousel of eight to twelve slides and its post text, from the material below.

- Author and what they know: [name and area of expertise]
- The process being taught: [topic]
- For whom: [role, seniority, situation]
- What the reader can do after the last slide: [the outcome]
- The steps, roughly in order: [step], [step], [step], ...
- Where people usually slip at each step: [mistake], [mistake], ... (optional)
- How long it takes in real life: [time or effort]
- A tool that helps: [name, or leave blank to stay tool-neutral]
- A real example from the author's own work: [what they did and what happened] (optional)

Slide plan:
- Slide 1: the outcome the reader will have by the end, as concretely as the material allows. Example shape: "Build a 50-account target list you trust in one afternoon."
- Slide 2: why most people skip this or do it badly, and what that costs, in two or three lines.
- One slide per step: the step number and a short name, two to four lines of what to do, a one-line warning about the usual slip, and one sentence of visual direction.
- Final slide: the steps again as a numbered list, then one specific request (save it, answer a question in the comments, or ask for a resource).

Post text: three to five short lines that promise the outcome and say who it is for.
```

## Rules for this draft

- Every step starts with something to do: "list", "write", "score", "send". Never "think about" or "consider".
- Written for professionals, not students: no throat-clearing, no definitions of the obvious.
- Any number, benchmark or result comes from the material. If a slide would benefit from one that was not supplied, write `[ASK: number and its source]`.
- Slide 1 has to work on its own in the feed.

## Check before handing back

- Could a reader follow the steps without the author in the room?
- Does each step slide fit in about six seconds of reading?
- Is the final request a single, concrete action?

## Hand back

The file path, the slide 1 line, and the gaps. The user designs the slides, exports a PDF and posts it themselves.
